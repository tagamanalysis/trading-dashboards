Swing Trading Rulebook — Complete Reference

6 locked, walk-forward-validated configurations · S&P 500 + NIFTY 500 · point-in-time universe, 1% risk/trade (1R = 1%)

Selection Methodology (applies to every config below)

1Full grid search, split by data slice

Each strategy's entry/exit parameter grid is swept independently per data slice (sp500_matched, nifty500_all_time — the two markets trimmed to the same ~13-year matched span so neither market's longer history skews the comparison). Every (entry, exit) combo is scored on train-era trades only.

2Winner selection

The combo with the highest drawdown_aware_score on train-era trades (minimum 30 train trades to qualify) is taken as the winner for that slice. Its train AND validation stats are both reported — validation trades were never seen by the selection process.

3Point-in-time universe correction

Every trade is checked against the actual index membership on its entry date (is_in_universe_on_date) — a stock that later joined or left the S&P 500 / NIFTY 500 can't generate a trade on a date it wasn't actually a constituent. Removes look-ahead/survivorship bias baked into a static current-day universe.

4Final 6 kept

Of 26 strategies tested, 4 were strong on both markets (RS-Leader, IBS, Fibonacci Pullback, Darvas Box) and kept for both slices. The best remaining 5th slot differs by market — Turtle/Donchian for NIFTY, Oversold Bounce for S&P — so both are kept, each restricted to the market it actually earned its place on. Every other strategy in the library was excluded for weaker/less consistent validation performance.

Strategy Mechanisms

1. RS-Leader (cross-sectional relative-strength breakout)

1Universe-wide RS ranking

Each ticker's trailing rs_lookback_months-month return is computed and turned into a percentile rank against every other ticker in the same universe on the same day (a threshold panel built once per slice).

2Leadership filter

Entry requires the ticker's own RS percentile to be at or above the universe's 80th-percentile threshold that day — it must actually be one of the market's current leaders, not just going up.

3Proximity + breakout trigger

Close must be within near_high_pct% of its own 52-week high, AND price must break above the prior base_lookback_weeks-week high on volume ≥ breakout_volume_mult× average.

4Exit

Hard stop at stop_max_pct% below entry, OR exit whenever the ticker's own RS percentile falls below rs_exit_percentile (leadership fading) — whichever comes first. No fixed price target; winners are allowed to run as long as relative strength holds.

2. IBS (Internal Bar Strength mean reversion)

1IBS calculation

IBS = (Close − Low) / (High − Low) — where in the day's range the close landed. Near 0 = closed near the low.

2Entry filters

Optional trend filter (close above 200-day EMA) and volume filter (volume above its 20-day average) narrow entries to pullbacks within an uptrend rather than every washed-out bar.

3Entry trigger

IBS < entry_ibs_threshold — a weak close relative to the day's own range.

4Exit

First bar where IBS > exit_ibs_threshold (a strong close signals the reversion has played out), or a 60-bar time-stop. No placed stop-loss — risk is measured against a nominal 1×ATR(14)-at-entry unit rather than an actual stop order.

3. Fibonacci Pullback (trend-continuation retracement)

1Impulse leg identification

A rolling-window swing high/low over the trailing impulse_lookback_weeks weeks approximates the most recent up-leg (documented proxy, not a true peak-finder).

2Trend filter

Close must be above the trend_ma_days-day SMA.

3Entry trigger

Price retraces into the fib_ratio level of that leg (within ±fib_tolerance_pct%) and resumes.

4Exit

Stop at the swing low minus a buffer, OR a fixed measured-move target (target_mult_of_leg × leg height) — whichever hits first. The fixed target caps upside, which is why avg win size runs lower than trend-riding strategies despite a healthy win rate.

4. Darvas Box (breakout from consolidation)

1Box detection

A rolling box_lookback_weeks-week high/low range approximates a Darvas consolidation box, capped at max_box_height_pct% to exclude ranges too wide to be a real base.

2Proximity filter

The box must sit within near_52w_high_pct% of the stock's 52-week high — breakouts from bases near new highs, not mid-range chop.

3Entry trigger

Close breaks above the box top on volume ≥ breakout_volume_mult× average.

4Exit

Stop at the box bottom minus a buffer, OR a fixed measured-move target (target_mult_of_box_height × box height) — whichever hits first. Same target-capping trade-off as Fibonacci Pullback.

5. Oversold Bounce (RSI mean reversion, uptrend only)

1Trend filter

Close must be above the trend_ma_length-day SMA — bounces are only taken with the larger trend, not against it.

2Entry trigger

Wilder RSI(oscillator_period) must actually cross up out of oversold — RSI[t-1] ≤ oversold_threshold AND RSI[t] > oversold_threshold. Being oversold alone is not sufficient; the bounce has to already be underway.

3Support confluence (optional)

When enabled, also requires close to be within 3% of the rolling 20-day low — a documented proxy for "at support," since the source material only specified this as a binary toggle without a concrete definition.

4Exit

RSI crossing back above exit_overbought_threshold, or a 60-bar time-stop. Same nominal 1×ATR(14) risk-unit convention as IBS — no placed stop.

6. Turtle/Donchian (classic trend-following breakout)

1Entry trigger

Close breaks above the prior entry_channel_length-bar Donchian channel high.

2Exit — whichever comes first

(a) an ATR-based stop fixed at entry (entry_price − atr_stop_mult × ATR(atr_lookback)), or (b) close falling below the trailing exit_channel_length-bar low.

3Classic System-1 filter

skip_if_last_winner — skip the next signal on a ticker if its previous trade was a winner (the original Turtle System 1 rule). Confirmed to have zero effect on the winning combo — True and False produce byte-identical train/val stats for these parameters, so it isn't doing any actual filtering here.

4Time cap

250-bar maximum hold as a safety cap. R is measured against the actual ATR-based stop distance at entry (a real placed stop, unlike IBS/Oversold Bounce).

Key Cross-Strategy Finding

Win rate and expectancy trade off in opposite directions between the trend-followers and the mean-reverters. RS-Leader and Turtle/Donchian post the lowest win rates (28–41%) but the highest PF and per-trade expectancy — they cut losers early and let a minority of trades do the compounding. IBS and Oversold Bounce invert this: 60%+ win rates but thinner per-trade edges, relying on trade count (IBS especially, with 9,000+ validation trades on NIFTY alone) to compound. Fibonacci Pullback and Darvas Box sit in between — both cap upside with a fixed measured-move target, which is why neither posts the standout expectancy of the two trend-followers despite solid win rates.
